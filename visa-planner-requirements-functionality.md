## 2. CRITICAL VISA CONDITION: 8558

### 2.1 The Rolling 18-Month Rule

**Condition 8558 States:**
> "Must not stay in Australia for more than 12 months in any 18-month period"

### 2.2 Understanding "Rolling 18-Month Period"

**What is a Rolling Period?**
- The 18-month window is NOT fixed to calendar dates
- It "rolls" continuously throughout the visa validity period
- At ANY point in time, looking back 18 months, the total stay cannot exceed 12 months
- Looking forward 18 months from any date, the cumulative stay cannot exceed 12 months

**Critical Examples:**

**Example 1: Continuous Stay Limit**
- If you stay for 12 months continuously, your visa **automatically ceases**
- You must then spend 6 months outside Australia before returning
- Formula: 18 months - 12 months stay = 6 months mandatory absence

**Example 2: Multiple Short Visits**
- Stay 1: 6 months (Jan-Jun 2025)
- Stay 2: 4 months (Aug-Nov 2025)  
- Stay 3: 3 months (Jan-Mar 2026)
- **Total in 18 months:** 13 months - **VIOLATION** (exceeds 12-month limit)

**Example 3: Valid Multiple Visits**
- Stay 1: 4 months (Oct 2024-Feb 2025)
- Gap: 5 months outside
- Stay 2: 5 months (Jul 2025-Dec 2025)
- Gap: 6 months outside
- Stay 3: 4 months (Jul 2026-Oct 2026)
- **Each 18-month window:** Maximum 9-10 months - **VALID**

### 2.3 Consequences of Violation

**Automatic Visa Cancellation If:**
- Continuous stay exceeds 12 months (automatic cessation)
- Cumulative stay in any 18-month period exceeds 12 months (visa cancellation)

**Long-Term Consequences:**
- Future visa applications may be denied
- Possible exclusion periods from Australia
- Compliance history affects all future applications

### 2.4 Re-entry Requirements

**After Extended Stay:**
If you have spent 12 months in Australia (or close to it), you must:
- Spend minimum 6 months outside Australia before applying for new visa
- New visa application assessed based on compliance history
- Cannot apply for new visa until sufficient time spent outside Australia

### 4.2 Complex Calculation Requirements

**Multiple Rolling Windows Must Be Checked:**

From the **first arrival date** (Oct 28, 2024):
- Window ending Apr 28, 2026 (18 months from first arrival)

From the **second arrival date** (Jul 27, 2025):
- Window ending Jan 27, 2027 (18 months from second arrival)

From any **extended departure date**:
- Window looking back 18 months
- Window looking forward 18 months

From any **future arrival date**:
- Window ending 18 months after that arrival

**The Critical Window** = The 18-month period with the MOST cumulative days

**High Risk Scenarios:**
- ⚠️ Extending current stay too long without accounting for past visits

**Low Risk Approach:**

## 5. VISA EXTENSION & RENEWAL CONSIDERATIONS

### 5.1 Current Visa Limits

**Hard Deadline:**
- All stays must conclude by **September 30, 2027**
- Cannot remain in Australia beyond this date on current visa

### 5.2 Applying for New Visa

**Important Clarification:**
> **A new visa does NOT reset the 18-month rolling window calculation**

**What This Means:**
- Even with a new visa, previous stays still count
- The rolling 18-month rule continues to apply from previous visa stays
- Immigration will consider your ENTIRE stay history, not just current visa

**Example:**
- Current visa expires: September 30, 2027
- You stayed: 11 months in the 18 months before visa expiry
- You apply for new 3-year visa in October 2027
- **Your 11 months still count** - you can only stay 1 month immediately
- Must wait for old stays to "roll out" of the 18-month window

### 5.3 Strategic Planning for Visa Extension

**Recommended Approach:**
1. Plan final visits under current visa to leave maximum flexibility
2. Ensure 6-month gap before applying for new visa (if recently stayed long period)
3. Understand that compliance history affects new visa approval
4. Consider Sponsored Parent Visa (Subclass 870) as alternative for longer stays (3-5 years continuous)

### 6.3 Calculation Methodology

#### 6.3.1 Windows Checked

The tool creates 18-month windows from multiple starting points:

**Window Types:**
1. **Forward windows** - From each stay's arrival date, extending 18 months forward
2. **Backward windows** - To each stay's departure date, looking 18 months back

**Example with 2 stays:**
- Window 1: From Stay #1 arrival (Oct 28, 2024 + 18 months)
- Window 2: From Stay #2 arrival (Jul 27, 2025 + 18 months)
- Window 3: To Stay #1 departure (Mar 22, 2025 - 18 months backward)
- Window 4: To Stay #2 departure (Jan 7, 2026 - 18 months backward)

#### 6.3.2 Overlap Calculation

For each window:
1. Determine window start and end dates (18-month span)
2. Check each stay for overlap with this window
3. Calculate overlapping days:
   - `overlap_start = MAX(stay_start, window_start)`
   - `overlap_end = MIN(stay_end, window_end)`
   - `overlap_days = overlap_end - overlap_start + 1` (if positive)
4. Sum all overlapping days from all stays
5. Compare total to 365-day limit

#### 6.3.3 Critical Window Identification

- The window with the **highest total days** is the "critical window"
- This window determines overall validity
- Even if other windows are fine, exceeding limit in ONE window = invalid

## 11. GLOSSARY OF TERMS

**Visa Validity Period:** The total duration the visa is valid (Oct 1, 2024 - Sep 30, 2027)

**Rolling 18-Month Period:** Any continuous 18-month timespan, which "rolls" forward throughout the visa validity

**Critical Window:** The 18-month period with the highest cumulative stay days

**Condition 8558:** Specific visa condition limiting stay to 12 months in any 18-month period

**Buffer:** Days remaining before reaching the 365-day limit in any window

**Cumulative Stay:** Total of all stay days within a specific time period

**Continuous Stay:** Unbroken stay without leaving Australia

**Compliance History:** Record of adherence to past visa conditions

**Stay Bar:** Visual representation of a visit on the timeline

**Drag Handle:** Interactive elements (⫷ ⫸) on stay bars for adjusting dates
